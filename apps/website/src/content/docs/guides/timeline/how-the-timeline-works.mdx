The timeline is the show in time. You set where marchers are at a count, and OpenMarch works out where they are on every count in between, so the field always shows the drill at the playhead's count.

## Counts and measures

A count is one beat of the show's music. The row under the pages numbers the measures, with a tick for each count, and shows rehearsal marks in small boxes. The waveform of the music runs under them.

Above the timeline, the readout says where the playhead is, for example "Pg 3 · ct 4/10" (count 4 of Page 3's 10 counts) and "m9 beat 4". The clock beside it gives the time into the music.

Counts come from the music: its tempo and its measures. [How counts and the music fit together](/guides/music/counts-and-music/) explains them, and [Line the counts up with the music](/guides/music/tempo/) shows how to change them.

## Pages

A page is a numbered stretch of counts that ends at a set, the picture a drill chart shows for that page. Each page is a box in the top row of the timeline, as long as its counts, with its number at its right end. That end is the page's flag: the count where marchers reach the page's set.

The box with a house at the start of the row is home, the start of the show, where every marcher stands before the first move.

Pages are labels on the music. They don't have to start on a downbeat, and you can add, split, lengthen and delete them without changing the music. See [Pages](/guides/timeline/pages/).

## The playhead and the start flag

The playhead is the line that marks the count the field shows. Drag it, or click the timeline, and the field follows. <Kbd action="previousPage" /> and <Kbd action="nextPage" /> step a page back or forward.

The start flag is the second line, with a pennant on top. It marks two things: where Play starts, and where marchers leave from when you move them.

The counts between the start flag and the playhead are shaded. They're the stretch you're editing, and the line of text over the field names it, for example "Editing Page 3's move". Select a page's box and the start flag goes to the start of the page and the playhead to its end, so you're editing that page.

## How marchers get from one set to the next

When you drag marchers on the field, you set where they are at the playhead's count. They leave from where they were at the start flag and walk to the new spot over the counts in between, in a straight line at an even step size. You don't place them on the counts in between: OpenMarch works out every count from the positions you set.

A position you set for a marcher at a count is a keyframe. Select marchers and the timeline marks their keyframes with diamonds along the bottom edge of the page row: filled where every selected marcher has one, hollow where only some do. Between the diamonds, a thin line marks where they stand still: solid where they're Held, dashed where they're Not defined.

On each page, a marcher is in one of three states:

- <Ui k="timeline.terms.defined" />: you've set its position on this page.
- <Ui k="timeline.terms.held" />: you've chosen to keep it still, where its last keyframe put it.
- <Ui k="timeline.terms.notDefined" />: nothing is decided yet, so it stays where it last was, and follows any change to that earlier position.

[Defined, Held, Not defined](/guides/timeline/defined-held-not-defined/) explains the difference and how to change a marcher's state.

## Moves

A move is a stretch of time in which marchers go from one place to another. Each page has a move of its own, which takes everyone who moves on that page from the page before to the page's set. You don't see it as a bar: the page box stands for it.

In Expert, a move can also start or end in the middle of a page, run across several pages, or overlap another move. Those moves have bars of their own under the music, with a name such as "Move 1", and you can resize, split, merge and rename them. A move you make over part of a page takes over from the page's move for its marchers and counts; the page's move still covers everyone else. See [Moves](/guides/timeline/moves/).

## Lanes

Under the page row and the count ruler, the timeline's rows are **lanes**: the move bars (<Ui k="timeline.lanes.moves" />) and a lane for each kind of cue the show uses, such as Equipment, who holds what over the show. The page row and the ruler always show: they're how you read time.

Only the lanes the show uses appear: Moves (in Expert, where moves have bars) and each cue lane the show has cues for. Giving performers equipment for the first time makes the Equipment lane appear.

Under the lane names on the left is the <Ui k="cues.lane.lanes" /> row. When some of the show's lanes are hidden, it says how many, such as "Lanes · 2 hidden". Click it for the list of lanes:

- Type in <Ui k="cues.lane.find" plain /> to filter the list.
- Under <Ui k="cues.lane.inThisShow" />, each lane has a check mark: clear it to hide the lane, tick it to show it again. Cue lanes show how many cues they have.
- Under <Ui k="cues.lane.addLane" />, the kinds of cue lanes the show doesn't use yet, grouped by family. Tick one to add it, empty and ready for cues. This part is left out when there's nothing to add.

You can also right-click a lane's name, or press and hold it on a tablet: <Ui k="cues.lane.hideNamed" plain /> hides it, <Ui k="cues.lane.collapseRows" plain /> or <Ui k="cues.lane.expandRows" plain /> folds a cue lane's rows into one, and <Ui k="cues.lane.lanes" plain /> opens the same list.

- Hiding Moves folds the move bars into a thin strip where they were, labeled <Ui k="timeline.lanes.movesHidden" />: click or tap the strip to show them again. The moves are still there, and the field shows and edits the drill as before.
- Any other lane, hidden, leaves nothing behind. A hidden lane with no cues goes back under <Ui k="cues.lane.addLane" plain />.
- Each lane's heading also has an eye-slash button that hides the lane where it is.
- The same choices are in the View menu and the command palette (<Kbd action="openCommandPalette" />), such as <Ui k="actions.cues.toggleMovesLane" />, where you can give each one a shortcut.

Which lanes show is kept on your computer, for every show you open.

## Basic and Expert

Both modes show the same show. They differ in where Play starts and in what the timeline lets you change:

| | Basic | Expert |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start flag | Not shown. Play starts at the start of the page the playhead is on. | Shown. You drag it to where Play should start; it lands on measures and page flags when it's near one, or on any count with <Kbd keys="Alt" gesture="drag" /> held. It stays there until you go to another page. |
| Moves | Each page has its move. A position in the middle of a page splits the page's move at that count. | Moves can start and end on any count, cross pages and overlap, and each has a bar. |
| Dragging the playhead | Lands on every count. | Lands on measures and page flags when it's near one. |
| Loop, isolating a move, resizing the timeline | Not available. | Available. |

The mode is a setting of the app, not of the show, so you can open any show in either mode. Basic plays everything made in Expert but locks what it can't edit. See [Basic and Expert](/guides/start/basic-and-expert/).

## On a tablet

On a touch screen, where a finger lands decides what it does:

- On the page boxes, or the measure numbers under them, one finger drags the playhead, as dragging with a mouse does. A tap on a page box selects it.
- On a move's bar, one finger drags the bar to new counts, as a mouse does.
- On empty space below the measure numbers, one finger scrolls the timeline sideways, and up and down when there are more rows than fit. A tap there moves the playhead to that count, as a click does.
- Two fingers scroll the timeline anywhere on it, and pinching with two fingers zooms it in and out around your fingers. The counts under your fingers stay under them, and spreading your fingers twice as far apart makes the counts twice as wide. Zooming out stops once the whole show fits.
- The **−** and **+** buttons in the row above the timeline, next to **…** (<Ui k="actions.timeline.zoomOut" /> and <Ui k="actions.timeline.zoomIn" />), zoom a step at a time, around the playhead when it's in view. [Play the show](/guides/timeline/play-the-show/#on-a-tablet) lists the rest of that row.

If a second finger touches the timeline while one finger is dragging the playhead or a bar, the drag is cancelled: the playhead or the bar goes back to where it was, and nothing changes. The two fingers then scroll and zoom.

The timeline's small handles don't need an exact touch. A finger that lands close to one, up to about a fingertip away, takes that handle:

- the start flag and the playhead;
- a page's flag, beside the line at the end of its box. The selected page's two flags are drawn, so you can see where to touch;
- the ends of a move's bar and its ⋯ button, once you tap the bar to select it. The selected bar shows its ends;
- the ends of a hold and the keyframe diamonds;
- the ends of the loop bar, which start to move once your finger has moved a little, so a tap that wobbles changes nothing;
- the divider above the timeline, from a little way above it.

A finger right on a handle takes that handle. Where your finger is between two handles, the one on the selected page or move wins, and otherwise the one nearest your finger. Where the start flag or the playhead sits on a page's flag, the line wins, as it does with a mouse: move the line off to drag the page's flag. A finger on a move's bar, or on another button on the timeline, takes that bar or button, except near the ends of the selected bar, which drag those ends. A dragged handle, other than the playhead, stays the same distance from your finger as when you touched it, so it doesn't jump under your finger.

A finger can't right-click, so the timeline's menus open two other ways:

- The selected page's box has a ⋯ button at its end. Select it to open the page's menu: the same items as the box's right-click menu, with <Ui k="timeline.moveMenu.edit" /> first, which a mouse gets by double-clicking the box.
- Hold a finger still on the playhead, a page's box, a move's bar or a keyframe diamond for half a second, then lift it: the menu a right-click there opens appears, and nothing else happens. A finger that shakes a little still counts. A finger that moves further, before or after the hold, drags as usual. Where a right-click has no menu, such as on the measure numbers, the touch is an ordinary tap.
