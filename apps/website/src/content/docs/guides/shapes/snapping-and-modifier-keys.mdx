## Land on yard lines, hashes and sidelines

When a moving corner, side or center comes near one of the field's lines, it lands on the line. The lines are the ones the coordinate sheets count from: yard lines, hashes and sidelines, and half lines if the field has them. This works on custom fields too.

- Drawing or stretching a box, the corner that moves lands on the line and the fixed side stays put. The spacing becomes whatever that takes and is kept exactly: 7 marchers across from the 40 to the 50 stand 2⅔ steps apart.
- Moving a shape, its nearest corner or its center lands on the line, side to side and front to back. Centering a block on the 50 or on a hash works this way.
- Dragging a box's spacing number lands a corner or the center on a line in the same way. Typing a number never snaps.

While a point is on a line, an orange guide runs across the field, named at the field's edge the way the coordinate sheets name it, such as "On 50" or "S1: On 40". A side lying along the line is drawn thicker, and a corner or center on it gets a ring. The guide clears when you let go.

The pull only reaches a few pixels on screen, so every half step near a line can still be reached. Circles don't catch the lines; they move and grow in half steps only.

## Turn snapping off

Select <Ui k="toolbar.alignment.snapToggleCaption" /> in the toolbar's <Ui k="inspector.shape.alignmentTab" /> tab, or press <Kbd action="toggleSnap" />. The same magnet button is in every shape's floating editor.

<Media
  id="shapes-snap-button"
  alt="The toolbar's Alignment tab: Coordinate Rounding, the two lock buttons, the Snap button's magnet lit in the accent color because snapping is off, and Snap to grid, four arrows pointing inward."
  maxWidth={520}
/>

While snapping is off:

- shapes you drag on the field go exactly where the pointer goes, off the half steps, and nothing pulls them onto lines or other marchers;
- shapes and groups of marchers turn freely, not in 15° steps;
- marchers you drag or nudge ignore coordinate rounding ([Move marchers](/guides/marchers/move-marchers/#snap-to-a-grid-of-steps) has the rest).

The button looks pressed while snapping is off, and a drag shows <Ui k="field.gestureChip.snapOff" /> by the pointer, so you can't forget it's off. Snapping comes back on when you restart OpenMarch or open a show. The timeline has its own snapping, which this doesn't change.

<Ui k="toolbar.alignment.snapCaption" />, beside it, is a different thing: it moves the selected marchers onto the nearest grid points, once (<Kbd action="snapToNearestCustomFraction" />).

## Hold a key to change a drag

Hold these keys during a drag on a shape. They take effect as soon as you press them, mid-drag, and stop when you let go. Holding two gives you both.

| Hold                                    | Role         | What it does                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <Kbd keys="Shift" gesture="drag" />     | Fine         | Off the half steps, in hundredths of a step, still catching yard lines and hashes. Turning goes by whole degrees. A dragged number in the floating editor changes in hundredths. |
| <Kbd keys="Mod" gesture="drag" />       | Constrain    | Drawing a box or dragging its corner makes the two spacings equal. Lines, curves and arcs draw at 45° steps. Moving keeps the shape on one axis. On a box's side handle or an arc's handles, it switches the proportion lock for that drag; on a line's end, it keeps the line's direction. |
| <Kbd keys="Alt" gesture="drag" />       | From center  | A box draws from its center: the press is its middle and the pointer a corner. Handles resize the shape about its center instead of holding the opposite side; a line's end moves both ends about the middle. |

<Kbd keys="Mod" /> is Command on a Mac and Ctrl elsewhere; <Kbd keys="Alt" /> is Option on a Mac. Ctrl on a Mac doesn't constrain: Control-click is a right-click there.

While you drag, a small label by the pointer names each key that does something there and what it does, such as "⌥ from center" or "⇧ fine". A key that does nothing for that drag isn't listed.

<Kbd keys="Mod" gesture="drag" /> from a shape's center dot moves its <Term id="pivot">pivot</Term>, as it does for a group of marchers ([Turn a group around another point](/guides/marchers/align-and-space/#turn-a-group-around-another-point)). Choose <Ui k="shapes.edit.pivot.backToCenter" /> in the floating editor's pivot menu to put it back.

## On a tablet

The buttons over the field stand in for these keys: <Ui k="touch.modifiers.fine" />, <Ui k="touch.modifiers.constrain" />, <Ui k="touch.modifiers.fromCenter" /> and <Ui k="touch.modifiers.noSnap" />. Hold one while you drag, or tap it to keep it on. See [The buttons over the field](/guides/workspace/on-a-tablet/#the-buttons-over-the-field).
