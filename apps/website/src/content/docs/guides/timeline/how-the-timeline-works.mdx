The timeline is the show in time. You set where marchers are at a count, and OpenMarch works out where they are on every count in between, so the field always shows the drill at the playhead's count.

The pictures in this chapter show Expert. Basic has the same timeline without the start flag and without bars for moves; [Basic and Expert](/guides/start/basic-and-expert/) compares the two modes.

## Counts and measures

A count is one beat of the show's music. The row under the pages numbers the measures, with a tick for each count, and shows rehearsal marks in small boxes (CO and ST in the picture). The music's waveform runs under them.

The readout above the timeline says where the playhead is: "Pg 3 · ct 10/10" is count 10 of Page 3's 10 counts, and "m11 beat 1" is the measure and beat. The clock beside it gives the time into the music.

Counts come from the music's tempo and measures; [How counts and the music fit together](/guides/music/counts-and-music/) explains them.

## Pages and their flags

A page is a numbered stretch of counts that ends at a set, the picture a drill chart shows for that page. Each page is a box in the top row of the timeline, as long as its counts, with its number at its right end. That end is the page's flag ("Page 3's flag"): the count where marchers reach the page's set.

The box with a house at the start of the row is home, the start of the show, where every marcher stands before the first move. Home is numbered 0, so the pages after it count up from Page 1, and a subset straight after home is 0A, as in the picture.

Pages are labels on the music: adding, lengthening or deleting one never changes the music. See [Add and change pages](/guides/timeline/pages/).

## The playhead and the start flag

The playhead is the line that marks the count the field shows. Drag it, or click the timeline, and the field follows. <Kbd action="previousPage" /> and <Kbd action="nextPage" /> step a page back or forward.

In Expert, the start flag is a second line, with a pennant on top. It marks two things: where Play starts, and where marchers leave from when you move them. The counts between the start flag and the playhead are shaded: they're the stretch you're editing, and a label beside the playhead gives their counts, such as "counts 5–10". In Basic there's no start flag: the start of the page the playhead is on does both jobs.

Over the field, the editing line names that stretch, for example "Editing Page 3's move", or in Basic "Editing page 3". Read it before you drag marchers: it says which page or move the drag changes.

What moves the start flag:

- Selecting a page's box puts the start flag at the start of the page and the playhead at its end, so you're editing that page. Going to a page with <Kbd action="nextPage" /> or <Kbd action="previousPage" /> does the same.
- Dragging the start flag moves it on its own.
- Clicking the timeline, dragging the playhead or stepping by counts moves only the playhead, as long as it stays after the start flag. Put the playhead before the start flag and the two swap: that count becomes the start flag, and the old start flag's count becomes the playhead. Put it on the start flag's own count and the playhead lands one count after it.

Every handle you drag on the timeline (the start flag, the playhead, a page's flag, the ends of a move, a hold or the loop) lands on a measure, a page's flag or the playhead when it's near one. Hold <Kbd keys="Alt" gesture="drag" /> to put it on any count. In Basic, the playhead lands on every count.

## How marchers get from one set to the next

When you drag marchers on the field, you set where they are at the playhead's count. They leave from where they were at the start flag and walk to the new spot over the counts in between, in a straight line at an even step size. You don't place them on the counts in between: OpenMarch works out every count from the positions you set.

<Media
  id="timeline-scrub"
  alt="The playhead is dragged from the end of Page 2 to the end of Page 3, and the marchers on the field walk from Page 2's set to Page 3's set as it moves."
/>

A position you set for a marcher at a count is a keyframe. On each page, a marcher is in one of three states:

- <Ui k="timeline.terms.defined" />: it has a keyframe on this page.
- <Ui k="timeline.terms.held" />: you've chosen to keep it still, where its last keyframe put it.
- <Ui k="timeline.terms.notDefined" />: nothing is decided yet, so it stays where it last was.

[Defined, Held, Not defined](/guides/timeline/defined-held-not-defined/) explains what each state does when you change an earlier page, and how the timeline marks them.

## Moves

A move is a stretch of time in which marchers go from one place to another. Each page has a move of its own, which takes everyone who moves on that page from the page before to the page's set. It has no bar: the page's box stands for it.

In Expert, a move can also start or end in the middle of a page, run across several pages, or cover only some marchers. Such a move has a bar of its own under the music, with a name such as "Move 1". For its marchers and counts it takes over from the page's move; the page's move still covers everyone else. See [Make and change moves](/guides/timeline/moves/).

## Lanes

Under the page row and the count ruler, the timeline's rows are **lanes**: the move bars (<Ui k="timeline.lanes.moves" />) and a lane for each kind of cue the show uses, such as Equipment, who holds what over the show. The page row and the ruler always show: they're how you read time.

Only the lanes the show uses appear: Moves (in Expert, where moves have bars) and each cue lane the show has cues for. Giving performers equipment for the first time makes the Equipment lane appear.

Under the lane names on the left is the <Ui k="cues.lane.lanes" /> row. When some of the show's lanes are hidden, it says how many, such as "Lanes · 2 hidden". Click it for the list of lanes:

- Type in <Ui k="cues.lane.find" plain /> to filter the list.
- Under <Ui k="cues.lane.inThisShow" />, each lane has a check mark: clear it to hide the lane, tick it to show it again. Cue lanes show how many cues they have.
- Under <Ui k="cues.lane.addLane" />, the kinds of cue lanes the show doesn't use yet, grouped by family. Tick one to add it, empty and ready for cues. This part is left out when there's nothing to add.

You can also right-click a lane's name, or press and hold it on a tablet: Hide and the lane's name, such as <Ui k="cues.lane.hideNamed" values={{ lane: "Equipment" }} plain />, hides it, <Ui k="cues.lane.collapseRows" plain /> collapses a cue lane's rows into one and <Ui k="cues.lane.expandRows" plain /> brings them back, and <Ui k="cues.lane.lanes" plain /> opens the same list.

- Hiding Moves folds the move bars into a thin strip where they were, labeled <Ui k="timeline.lanes.movesHidden" />: click or tap the strip to show them again. The moves are still there, and the field shows and edits the drill as before.
- Any other lane, hidden, leaves nothing behind. A hidden lane with no cues goes back under <Ui k="cues.lane.addLane" plain />.
- Each lane's heading also has an eye-slash button that hides the lane where it is.
- The same choices are in the View menu and the command palette (<Kbd action="openCommandPalette" />), such as <Ui k="actions.cues.toggleMovesLane" />, where you can give each one a shortcut.

Which lanes show is kept on your computer, for every show you open.

## On a tablet

One finger on the page boxes or the measure numbers drags the playhead; on the empty space below them it scrolls the timeline. Touch and hold opens the menu a right-click would. Zooming and scrolling are in [Zoom, scroll and resize the timeline](/guides/timeline/play-the-show/#zoom-scroll-and-resize-the-timeline), and [Use OpenMarch on a tablet](/guides/workspace/on-a-tablet/) has the rest.
