<InShort>In the toolbar's <Ui k="inspector.shape.fileTab" plain /> tab, select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.exportButton" />, then the <Ui k="exportCoordinates.video" /> tab, then <Ui k="exportCoordinates.export" />.</InShort>

A video export plays the whole show on the field from above, from the first count to the end of the last page, with the music. Marchers and staff can watch the drill without OpenMarch. It works in the desktop app and in the web editor.

<Steps>

1. In the toolbar's <Ui k="inspector.shape.fileTab" plain /> tab, select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.exportButton" />, then the <Ui k="exportCoordinates.video" /> tab. In the web editor, <Ui k="web.fileMenu" plain /> › <Ui k="actions.file.exportVideo" /> in the title bar opens the same tab.

2. Choose the video's settings and what it shows (below).

3. Select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.export" />. In the desktop app, choose where to save the video. In the web editor, the video is made first; select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoWebSave" /> when it's finished.

</Steps>

OpenMarch draws the video frame by frame, which can take several minutes for a long show. When it's done, select the message that appears to open the video.

## Video settings

<Media
  id="files-video-settings"
  alt="The top of the Export dialog on the Video tab: the Mobile, Coordinate Sheets, Drill Charts and Video tabs, then Resolution 1080p, Frame rate 60 fps and Video background Light or Dark, and the On-screen info heading above the preview."
/>

- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoResolution" />: 720p, 1080p or 4K. The desktop app starts on 1080p, or 720p on a tablet. The web editor offers 720p and 1080p and starts on 720p.
- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFrameRate" />: 30 or 60 frames a second. 60 makes smoother motion and takes longer to make. The desktop app starts on 60 and the web editor on 30.
- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoBackground" />: a light or dark field.

## Choose what the video shows

Under <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoOverlay" plain />, the preview shows one frame of the video, with a box of information over the field. Choose what the box shows:

- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoOverlaySetCounts" />: the page, or the two pages a move is between, and the count.
- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoOverlayMeasures" />: the measure number and rehearsal mark. It's off when the show has no measures.
- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoOverlayTempo" />: the tempo in beats per minute.
- <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoOverlayClock" />: the time since the start of the show.

## Move the information box and the field

In the preview, drag the box to move it, drag the handle on its side to change its width, and drag its corner to change its size. Drag an empty part of the preview to move the field, and scroll to zoom.

<Media
  id="files-video-overlay"
  alt="In the video preview, the information box is dragged from the bottom left up the field, then its corner handle is dragged to make it larger."
/>

The <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFieldFraming" plain /> sliders (<Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFieldScale" plain />, <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFieldOffsetX" plain /> and <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFieldOffsetY" plain />) move the field in small steps, and <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoFieldResetFraming" /> puts it back.

## What else goes in the video

The video uses the music file selected in the show's music; the line under the options names it. With no music file, the video is silent. A small OpenMarch mark sits in a corner of the video. Props aren't drawn.

If a move runs past the last page, the dialog says so; select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.addPagesToEnd" /> to include it ([Moves past the end of the show](/guides/files/print-charts/#moves-past-the-end-of-the-show)).

## In the web editor

Making a video in a browser depends on the browser:

- It needs Safari 16.4 or later, Chrome or Edge. In some browsers the video has no sound; the dialog says so before you start.
- Keep OpenMarch open and the screen on until the video is done. Switching apps or locking the screen may stop it.
- When it's done, select <Ui k="exportCoordinates.videoWebSave" /> to download it. On an iPad or iPhone, choose **Save Video** to put it in Photos, or **Save to Files**.

If the dialog says video export needs WebGL, the browser isn't drawing with the graphics card. Turn on hardware acceleration (in Chrome or Edge, **Use graphics acceleration when available** in the browser's System settings) or update your graphics drivers, then try again.
