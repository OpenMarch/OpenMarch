<InShort>Put the playhead where the prop first appears, select <Ui k="props.title" /> (the cube) in the sidebar, choose an outline such as <Ui k="props.outline.rectangle" plain />, and draw it on the field.</InShort>

A prop is a set piece on the field: a riser, a platform, a floor tarp, a wall or a backdrop. You draw its outline on the field and give it a height, or [import a 3D model](#import-a-3d-model) of it, and move it during the show the way you move marchers. The 3D View draws a drawn prop as a plain block, and an imported one as its model.

The pit's instruments, speakers and podiums aren't props: see [Set up the front ensemble](/guides/3d-view/front-ensemble/).

## Draw a prop

<Steps>

1. Move the playhead to the count where the prop first appears on the field.

2. Select <Ui k="props.title" /> (the cube) in the sidebar.

3. Under <Ui k="props.draw.title" plain />, choose an outline: <Ui k="props.outline.rectangle" />, <Ui k="props.outline.circle" />, <Ui k="props.outline.arc" />, <Ui k="props.outline.polygon" /> or <Ui k="props.outline.freehand" />. The panel closes and a note at the bottom of the field says how to draw that outline.

4. Draw the outline on the field:
   - <Ui k="props.outline.rectangle" plain />: drag from one corner to the opposite corner.
   - <Ui k="props.outline.circle" plain />: drag from the center out to the edge.
   - <Ui k="props.outline.arc" plain />: click both ends of the arc, then click to set how far it curves.
   - <Ui k="props.outline.polygon" plain />: click each corner, then double-click the last one to finish.
   - <Ui k="props.outline.freehand" plain />: drag along the shape you want.

</Steps>

<Media
  id="3d-props-draw"
  alt="Rectangle is chosen in the Props panel, the panel closes and a note at the bottom of the field says to drag, then a drag in the open field behind the band draws a rectangle prop about ten yards wide."
/>

The prop appears as soon as you finish, and the drawing tool turns off. To stop drawing without making a prop, select <Ui k="props.draw.cancel" /> in the note or press <Kbd keys="Escape" />.

<Media
  id="3d-props-polygon"
  alt="Polygon is chosen in the Props panel, then three clicks near the back sideline set three corners and a double-click sets the fourth and finishes a four-sided prop."
/>

Corners and ends snap to the coordinate rounding grid, as dragged marchers do ([Snap to a grid of steps](/guides/marchers/move-marchers/#snap-to-a-grid-of-steps)). Freehand outlines don't snap.

A new prop is on the field from the playhead's count, not before. It starts as a <Ui k="props.surface.floor" plain /> prop with no height, like a tarp; give it a height (below) to make it a riser or a wall.

On a tablet, tap the corners of an arc or a polygon. The note's <Ui k="props.draw.undoPoint" /> takes the last one back, and for a polygon <Ui k="props.draw.finish" /> ends it once it has three corners.

## Import a 3D model

You can bring in a model of your own, such as a chair, a door frame or a sculpture, as a `.glb` file, as a `.gltf` file with the files it names (its `.bin` and pictures), or as the `.zip` a model site such as Sketchfab downloads. There's no need to unzip it.

<Steps>

1. Select <Ui k="props.title" /> in the sidebar.

2. Under <Ui k="props.imported.title" plain />, select <Ui k="props.imported.import" />, and choose the model's files. You can also drop the files anywhere on the window.

3. Check the model on the review that opens in the panel (below), and change what's wrong.

4. Select <Ui k="props.import.add" />.

</Steps>

The model appears under <Ui k="props.imported.title" plain /> with its picture and its footprint. It's stored in the show file, so the show opens with it on any computer. Adding it doesn't place it yet: see [Place an imported model](#place-an-imported-model).

### The review

- The picture shows the model from the audience, a little to one side. Its front should face you, right side up. The import stands it the way the file says, which is right for most models. If it doesn't face you, or lies on its side or back, rotate it a quarter turn at a time: <Ui k="props.import.rotateAxis" values={{ axis: "Y" }} /> turns it like a turntable, <Ui k="props.import.rotateAxis" values={{ axis: "X" }} /> brings its top toward you, and <Ui k="props.import.rotateAxis" values={{ axis: "Z" }} /> lays its top to your right. The small drawing in the corner shows the three axes; point at a button, or tap it, and its axis lights up with an arrow showing which way the model will swing.
- The size is the model's real size, in meters. If it's wrong, the file was made in another unit: under <Ui k="props.import.units.label" plain />, choose the one it was made in. When the size is far off, the import has already picked the likely unit, and says so.
- <Ui k="props.import.detail.title" plain />, for a model with more than 20,000 triangles: <Ui k="props.import.detail.levels.light" /> simplifies it to about 20,000 so the 3D View stays smooth, and is chosen for you. <Ui k="props.import.detail.levels.detailed" /> (for a model over 100,000) keeps about 100,000, and <Ui k="props.import.detail.levels.original" /> keeps every triangle. Under the choice, the review says how many triangles the model keeps, how much it adds to the show file, and how many performers one copy of it is as heavy to draw as. Above Light, the show also keeps a Light copy: if the 3D View stutters, it draws the Light copies first, before it lowers the quality of everything else.
- <Ui k="props.import.compare.open" /> shows the model in 3D, to compare the level you chose with the original (or, at Original, with the Light copy) and with the <Ui k="props.import.compare.farCopy" plain />: the much simpler copy the 3D View draws once the camera is far from the prop. The bigger the prop, the farther out it keeps its detail: a 1.5 m prop switches at about 30 m, a 4.5 m car at about 90 m, and none later than 100 m. Drag to turn it, scroll to zoom, and switch versions to see what simplifying changed. <Ui k="props.import.compare.stands" plain /> moves the camera 30 m away, about where the audience sees props from.

<Media
  id="3d-props-import-compare"
  alt="The Compare in 3D window: a switch between Light, Original and Far-away copy, the red chair at its Original level turned a little, the hint Drag to turn, scroll to zoom, and From the stands."
  maxWidth={600}
/>

- <Ui k="props.import.groups.fixed" plain /> lists what the import changed on its own, such as pictures made smaller. Select it to see the list. An animated model, such as a character with a skeleton, comes in standing still: its animations and rig are removed.
- <Ui k="props.import.name" plain /> is what the model is called. Props you place from it are numbered after it: Chair 1, Chair 2.
- <Ui k="props.import.credits.title" plain /> holds the license, author, source and attribution, filled in from the file when it says. Leave them empty if you don't know; empty means unknown. Only import models you have the rights to use.

Each change runs the import again, so the picture and size always show what <Ui k="props.import.add" plain /> stores. <Ui k="props.import.cancel" /> leaves the show as it was.

If a file can't be imported, the review says why under <Ui k="props.import.groups.cantImport" plain />, for example a `.gltf` whose `.bin` wasn't chosen with it, or a model far too detailed. When the import itself failed rather than the file, <Ui k="props.import.tryAgain" /> runs it again.

## Place an imported model

<Steps>

1. Move the playhead to the count where the prop first appears on the field.

2. In the Props panel, under <Ui k="props.imported.title" plain />, select the model. The panel closes.

3. Click the field where the prop goes (on a tablet, tap).

</Steps>

The prop takes the model's footprint and height, and from then on it's like any other prop: drag it, turn it with its handles, and move it during the show. Undo (<Kbd action="performUndo" />) removes the prop and keeps the model under <Ui k="props.imported.title" plain />. A model with height is an <Ui k="props.surface.obstacle" plain /> prop; a flat one is a <Ui k="props.surface.floor" plain /> prop.

You can also find both steps in the command palette: <Ui k="actions.items.importModel" plain /> and <Ui k="actions.items.placeImportedModel" plain />.

The field draws an imported prop as the model seen from above, with a small mark on its front once you zoom in close, and the 3D View draws the model itself. An imported prop keeps the model's shape. Dragging a side or corner handle resizes it evenly, and in its settings Width, Depth and Height change together. A flat model, such as a rug, keeps its width and depth in step and lets its height change on its own. While the model loads in the 3D View, or if the show doesn't have it, the prop draws there as a block.

## The show's imported models

Every model you import is stored in the show file, whether or not a prop uses it. To see them, select <Ui k="props.imported.manage" /> under <Ui k="props.imported.title" plain /> in the Props panel, or find <Ui k="actions.ui.openAssets" plain /> in the command palette. The <Ui k="assets.title" plain /> view lists each model with its picture, its size in the show file, how many times the show uses it, and its license.

<Media
  id="3d-props-assets"
  alt="The Assets view: a note that 1 asset isn't used anywhere in the show, 29 KB, with a Remove unused… button, then two 3D models: a chair, 3.9 MB, used once, whose license is unknown so you may not have the right to share it, and a test pattern, 29 KB, not used, licensed CC-BY-4.0."
  maxWidth={480}
/>

- Select a model to see and change its credits: license, author, source and attribution. Changing them doesn't change the model or the props that use it.
- A model whose license is unknown says you may not have the right to share it. Add its license if you know it.
- When some models aren't used by any prop, a note at the top says how many and how much space they take. <Ui k="assets.removeUnused.button" /> lists them and removes them only when you confirm. Nothing is ever removed on its own, not when you save or close the show. Undo doesn't bring a removed model back.
- If an undo brings back a prop whose model you removed, the prop draws as a block, and both the note at the top and the prop's settings say its model is missing. To replace it, import the model again, place it as a new prop and delete the old one.
- When imported models make the show file large (over 500 MB), the note says so: large shows are slower to open, save and share.

## Change a prop's name, color and size

<Steps>

1. Right-click the prop on the field and choose the edit item, which carries the prop's name, such as <Ui k="actions.items.editNamed" values={{ name: "Riser" }} />. Or select <Ui k="props.title" /> in the sidebar, then <Ui k="props.edit" /> (the pencil) next to the prop.

2. Change what you want in the <Ui k="props.editTitle" plain /> panel.

</Steps>

<Media
  id="3d-props-edit"
  alt="The Edit Prop panel: Name Riser, Locked off, Color None, Surface Floor, and under Outline from count 20, Width 6 m, Depth 2 m, Height 1.2 m, Rotation 0°, Transition 1 counts, and Roll-out set to Doesn't roll out."
  maxWidth={480}
/>

- <Ui k="props.name" />: shown on the field next to the prop when names are shown. Without a name, the prop goes by its number.
- <Ui k="props.lockLabel" />: on, the prop can't be selected or moved on the field ([Lock a prop](#lock-a-prop)). Off, the default for a prop you draw, it moves like a marcher (below).
- <Ui k="props.color" />: colors the prop on the field and in the 3D View. <Ui k="props.colorNone" plain /> goes back to the plain look. To color several props at once, select them on the field: the Props panel shows one color for all of them.
- <Ui k="props.surfaceLabel" />: <Ui k="props.surface.floor" plain />, <Ui k="props.surface.obstacle" plain /> or <Ui k="props.surface.platform" plain />. In the 3D View, marchers and props over a <Ui k="props.surface.platform" plain /> prop stand on it. On an imported model they stand on the top surface the import found over its middle, when it found one, rather than at its full height.
- <Ui k="props.width" />, <Ui k="props.depth" /> and <Ui k="props.height" />, in meters, and <Ui k="props.rotation" /> in degrees clockwise.
- <Ui k="props.rollOut.label" /> (floor props only): which edge a tarp rolls out from. It's saved with the prop, but in this version it doesn't change how the prop is drawn.

### Grow, shrink or turn a prop during the show

The size and turn are set at a count. The heading <Ui k="props.outlineFrom" values={{ count: "N" }} plain /> names the count the playhead is on, and a change you make goes on that count. The prop keeps its earlier size until then; if it already has a change on that count, yours replaces it.

<Steps>

1. With the prop's <Ui k="props.editTitle" plain /> panel open, move the playhead to the count where the change should be complete.

2. Change <Ui k="props.width" plain />, <Ui k="props.depth" plain />, <Ui k="props.height" plain /> or <Ui k="props.rotation" plain />.

3. Set <Ui k="props.transition" /> to the number of counts the change takes. With 8, the prop turns or resizes over the 8 counts up to that count; with 1, it changes on the count.

</Steps>

Before the prop's first count, the panel says the prop is off the field, and you can't change its size there.

## Resize and turn a prop on the field

A selected prop shows no handles, so a drag anywhere on it moves it. To resize or turn it, right-click it and choose <Ui k="actions.items.resize" />: its handles appear until you select something else.

Drag a side or a corner handle to resize it, as you would a group of marchers: the opposite side stays where it is, so the prop's center moves with the resize. Drag the round handle to turn it about its center. Like the panel, a resize or turn is saved at the playhead's count. A resize that moves the prop is saved like a drag of the prop, in the same undo step: on a later page the prop moves there during that page's move, and where a drag would be refused the resize is too. Hold <Kbd keys="Alt" gesture="drag" /> to resize it without moving it.

Hold these keys while you drag a side or corner handle:

- <Kbd keys="Alt" gesture="drag" /> resizes the prop about its center, so it doesn't move and is saved at the playhead's count alone.
- <Kbd keys="Mod" gesture="drag" /> keeps the prop's proportions.
- <Kbd keys="Shift" gesture="drag" /> resizes it slowly, for fine changes.

While the handles show, a drag on one resizes or turns the prop, even where it covers a small prop; a drag inside the prop away from them still moves it. To move a small prop the handles cover, select something else, then drag the prop.

## Move a prop during the show

A prop moves the way a marcher does. Select it and drag it on a later page, or use the alignment tools ([Move marchers](/guides/marchers/move-marchers/)). It travels there during that page's <Term id="move">move</Term>, and it's held in place when nothing moves it. Props aren't in the marcher list.

Select props together with marchers and they share the same tools. A [shape](/guides/shapes/) gives a prop a spot like any marcher, a path takes it along with the others, and align, distribute and swap treat it as one more dot. A box or a lasso picks props up with marchers, and so does <Ui k="actions.select.selectAll" plain />. To select every prop, open the <Ui k="inspector.shape.selectTab" plain /> tab, set <Ui k="toolbar.select.selectByText" plain /> to <Ui k="toolbar.select.selectBy.section" plain /> and choose <Ui k="section.prop" plain /> ([Select marchers](/guides/marchers/select-marchers/#select-a-whole-section-tag-family-or-drill-prefix)).

A [locked](#lock-a-prop) prop stays out of all of this.

## Lock a prop

Lock a prop that should stay where it is while you work around it, such as a tarp the band marches over. Right-click it and choose the lock item, which carries its name, such as <Ui k="actions.items.lockNamed" values={{ name: "Tarp" }} />.

A locked prop can't be clicked, boxed, lassoed or selected with <Ui k="actions.select.selectAll" plain />, and no tool moves it, so you can select and move the marchers on and around it. It keeps any moves it has, and still moves during the show where they say. Clicking it shows a <Ui k="field.lockedItem.tag" plain /> tag beside the pointer for a few seconds; click the tag for the same menu a right-click opens. The Props panel marks a locked prop with a lock.

To change it again, right-click it and choose <Ui k="actions.items.unlockNamed" values={{ name: "Tarp" }} />, or <Ui k="actions.items.editNamed" values={{ name: "Tarp" }} /> to open its settings. The pit's equipment starts locked ([Set up the front ensemble](/guides/3d-view/front-ensemble/)).

## Hide or delete a prop

The Props panel lists every prop:

- The eye next to a prop hides it on the field and in the 3D View, or shows it again. Hiding is a view setting on your computer; it doesn't change the show.
- <Ui k="props.showNames" /> shows or hides every prop's name on the field.
- Select a prop's row to select the prop on the field.
- <Ui k="props.delete" /> (the trash can) deletes the prop. Undo (<Kbd action="performUndo" />) brings it back.

## Props in the 3D View

In the 3D View each drawn prop is a solid block: its outline raised to its height, in its color or plain gray. A prop with no height is a thin slab on the field. Blocks move, turn and resize with the timeline, and have no pictures or textures. An imported prop is drawn as its model, with its own colors and textures, and moves, turns and resizes the same way. Props aren't in exports: not on coordinate sheets, drill charts or videos, and not sent to On the Move.
