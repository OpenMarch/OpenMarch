Add a recording of the show's music so it plays with the drill and its waveform shows on the timeline. The recording is saved inside the show file, so it travels with the show to another computer.

OpenMarch opens MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A, AAC and WebM files.

## Add a music file

<Steps>

1. Select <Ui k="tempoMode.button.text" /> in the timeline's controls (<Kbd keys="Shift+T" />). The timeline changes to Edit music, with its tools along the top.
2. Select <Ui k="tempoMode.music.add" />. If the show already has music, select <Ui k="tempoMode.import.button" /> and then <Ui k="tempoMode.import.audio" /> instead.
3. Choose the file.

</Steps>

The file's name appears next to <Ui k="tempoMode.toolbar.title" />, and its waveform fills the <Ui k="tempoMode.lanes.music.name" /> lane. The new file becomes the one the show plays. Select <Ui k="tempoMode.toolbar.done" /> or press <Kbd keys="Escape" /> to go back to the timeline.

You can also add music from the command palette (<Kbd action="openCommandPalette" />) with <Ui k="actions.file.insertAudio" />, and when you create a new show.

## Switch to another recording

A show can keep several recordings, such as a full-band recording and a MIDI mock-up. Only one plays at a time.

<Steps>

1. In Edit music, select the music file's name.
2. Choose the recording you want. A check mark shows the one that plays.

</Steps>

## Set where the music starts

The music offset is how many seconds after count 1 the music starts. Use it when the recording has silence or a count-off before the first note, or when the drill starts before the music does.

<Steps>

1. In Edit music, select the music file's name, then <Ui k="tempoMode.music.offset" />. The menu shows the current offset.
2. Enter the offset in seconds. A positive number starts the music after count 1; a negative number skips that much of the start of the recording.
3. Select <Ui k="tempoMode.music.offsetSet" />.

</Steps>

The waveform slides under the counts. The offset is part of the show's history, so <Kbd action="performUndo" /> puts it back. The same number is <Ui k="workspaceSettings.audioOffsetSeconds" /> in [Show settings](/guides/field/show-settings/).

To make the first hit land on a count, play the opening with the metronome on (<Kbd action="toggleMetronome" />) and change the offset until the click and the hit sound together. Then line up the rest of the counts with the tempo: see [Line the counts up with the music](/guides/music/tempo/).

## Delete a recording

<Steps>

1. In Edit music, select the music file's name, then <Ui k="tempoMode.music.delete" />.
2. Select <Ui k="music.delete" />.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">Deleting a recording can't be undone. Keep a copy of the audio file if you might want it again.</Aside>

With no recording left, the show plays in silence. The counts and drill don't change.

## Change the volume

Select the speaker button in the timeline's controls. Its slider sets the volume of the music and the metronome together, and the speaker icon next to the slider (<Ui k="timeline.sound.mute" />) silences the music while the metronome keeps counting.

## Change how the waveform looks

Open settings (<Kbd action="openSettings" />), choose <Ui k="settings.timeline" />, and under <Ui k="settings.timeline.waveformStyle" /> pick a style:

- <Ui k="settings.timeline.waveformStyle.simplified" />: rounded bars at an even spacing, scaled to the loudest moment.
- <Ui k="settings.timeline.waveformStyle.precise" />: square bars that line up with each count, scaled so quiet passages show their detail.
- <Ui k="settings.timeline.waveformStyle.hidden" />: no waveform, so the timeline takes less room. The music still plays.

The <Ui k="tempoMode.lanes.music.name" /> lane in Edit music always shows the waveform.
